```text
You are my creative director, contemplative experience designer, symbolic researcher, generative artist, and senior Three.js engineer. Help me design and build a coherent series of immersive browser artworks that guide a person inward.

## Core vision

I want to create art that helps people move beneath ordinary mental noise and explore the archetypes of Mind, Body, and Spirit. The work should point people toward their real self—not toward ego, self-importance, or a prescribed identity, but toward inward recognition, presence, unconditional love, and the felt sense of the Creator within all things.

This is not religious instruction, conversion, doctrine, therapy, or a claim to reveal absolute truth. Do not tell visitors what to believe or what an experience “means.” Create open symbolic spaces in which they can notice, feel, choose, and discover for themselves. The experience should be soothing for the heart and mind, spacious enough for mystery, and gentle enough that silence remains part of the artwork.

A key emotional inspiration came from seeing Rosalía in concert. She seemed to carry so much light. I want to translate that quality—not her likeness, branding, music, or stage design, but the feeling of a human presence radiating aliveness, beauty, courage, tenderness, and light—into immersive art that helps others remember their own inner light.

The central creative question is:

How can a digital environment use space, motion, sound, symbolism, and choice to help someone leave the noise of the outer world, encounter the architecture of their inner world, and return with a quieter awareness of who they really are?

## Source material and symbolic framework

Use the Law of One / L/L Research channelings, especially Ra and Q’uo, as contemplative source material. The principal structure is the tarot archetypal mind discussed by Ra:

- Mind: Matrix, Potentiator, Catalyst, Experience, Significator, Transformation, Great Way
- Body: Matrix, Potentiator, Catalyst, Experience, Significator, Transformation, Great Way
- Spirit: Matrix, Potentiator, Catalyst, Experience, Significator, Transformation, Great Way

Treat these twenty-one stations as a living architecture for contemplation, not as a rigid taxonomy to lecture about. Preserve the distinction between Mind, Body, and Spirit and the seven functions within each cycle. Do not casually merge Ra’s meanings with conventional fortune-telling tarot meanings.

When interpreting this material:

- Ground claims in source excerpts or documents I provide.
- Clearly distinguish direct source meaning, your inference, and free artistic interpretation.
- Never invent quotations, session numbers, or doctrinal certainty.
- Prefer experiential translation over explanation: turn a relationship into movement, light, resistance, reflection, sound, distance, rhythm, or choice.
- Let symbols remain multivalent. Do not reduce them to one answer.
- Use Q’uo as a tonal and contemplative complement—especially themes of seeking, acceptance, polarity, love, silence, and the mystery of self—without forcing every scene into explicit exposition.

The visitor should not need prior knowledge of the Law of One. The work must stand on its own as art. Optional interpretive notes may exist outside the central experience, but the experience itself should communicate through sensation and discovery.

## Visual language

Use the attached images of my original ink drawings as the primary aesthetic reference. Study their visual grammar rather than merely placing them in the background. Extract qualities such as:

- intricate fine-line geometry;
- hand-drawn white or warm-gold linework emerging from darkness;
- spirals, circles, lattices, rays, woven paths, symbolic maps, and sacred-geometric rhythms;
- language and marks becoming architecture;
- asymmetry inside larger coherence;
- the feeling of a diagram that is also a prayer, portal, or inner landscape.

Translate that grammar into living three-dimensional space. The drawings should feel as if they have unfolded into an explorable cosmos: lines become paths, circles become thresholds, mandalas become worlds, written currents become particles, and small imperfections keep the environment human.

The mood is ethereal calm: midnight blue, charcoal, pearl white, dim gold, occasional blush or iridescent color, soft bloom, deep negative space, translucent forms, slow breathing motion, gentle fog, distant points of light, and forms that feel etheric rather than metallic or mechanical.

Avoid generic “spiritual” clichés: no stock chakra rainbow, no literal heaven, no guru figure, no angel wings, no forced lotus imagery, no New Age interface, no religious iconography used as decoration, and no wall of explanatory text. Avoid gamification, scores, achievements, urgency, and overstimulation. The experience is an invitation, not a test.

## Experience principles

Design for inward attention, not visual spectacle alone.

1. **Arrival before instruction.** Let sound, darkness, breath-like motion, and one simple gesture establish safety and presence before asking the visitor to choose anything.
2. **Agency without pressure.** The visitor may pause, wander, return, or do nothing. Choices should reveal relationships, not reward a “correct” answer.
3. **Meaning through interaction.** A symbol should change through proximity, patience, orientation, stillness, touch, or release—not through explanatory labels.
4. **Stillness is active.** Reward slowing down. Some details should appear only when the visitor stops moving or holds attention.
5. **Contrast without moralism.** Light and shadow, attraction and resistance, conscious and unconscious, separation and union are complementary conditions for discovery—not simplistic good-versus-evil scoring.
6. **Embodiment matters.** Motion, scale, rhythm, spatial resistance, and sound should make the Body sequence genuinely felt rather than treating the whole project as abstract thought.
7. **Silence belongs.** Sound must leave air around itself. Give visitors control over audio and never autoplay harshly.
8. **Return matters.** Each piece should end—or gently open—into integration, not trap the visitor in endless stimulation.

## Six-piece series

Build a coherent family of six distinct, complete experiences. Each must work independently, while shared visual and sonic motifs make the series feel like one inward journey.

### 1. Ether Crossing

Purpose: the threshold from outer noise into inner space.

Begin in near-darkness with a single breathing point or fine-line sigil. A press-and-hold, sustained touch, or patient stillness opens the threshold. The visitor passes slowly through a tunnel of hand-drawn geometric filaments, fragments of mapped language, and responsive particles. Speed decreases as attention settles. The destination is not a grand reveal but an open, quiet ether: spacious, luminous, and alive. Establish the project’s core interaction vocabulary and offer entrances toward Mind, Body, and Spirit without turning them into a menu screen.

### 2. Veil of the Unconscious

Purpose: explore the relation between Matrix and Potentiator, especially the unknown depth behind conscious awareness.

Create a translucent veil, field, or horizon that cannot be conquered by force. Direct pursuit makes it recede or become opaque; peripheral movement, waiting, softness, or indirect attention allows hidden forms to appear. Reflections should be partial and changing. The unconscious is not a monster or puzzle box but a fertile mystery. Use shadow, water-like refraction, submerged geometry, and nearly heard tones. The visitor learns through encounter that receptivity reveals what control cannot.

### 3. The Star Within

Purpose: approach the Spirit through vulnerability, hope, inward illumination, and unforced radiance.

A dark expanse contains a faint inner star that initially seems distant. It becomes clearer not by collecting external light but by becoming still, releasing motion, or aligning scattered fragments. Light should feel intimate rather than triumphant. A simple act—pouring, opening, breathing, or letting particles pass through—can reveal that the source of illumination was also within the visitor’s field. Keep this tender, spacious, and free of savior imagery.

### 4. The Lovers’ Choice

Purpose: embody Transformation of the Mind as conscious orientation and chosen relationship.

Present a living crossroads or divided current, but avoid a crude binary morality test. Different directions may represent control versus acceptance, possession versus communion, concealment versus honesty, or separation versus mutual recognition. The environment should respond to sustained orientation: repeated small choices gradually organize the surrounding geometry. Include attraction, ambiguity, and consequence. The piece should ask, without text-heavy explanation: What do you choose to serve, and what kind of self does that choice create?

### 5. Three Islands

Purpose: let visitors experience Mind, Body, and Spirit as distinct but interdependent terrains.

Create three explorable islands or realms in an infinite ether:

- Mind: floating forests, libraries without books, mirrors, fog, branching paths, language becoming geometry.
- Body: mountains, gravity, pulse, warmth, weight, cycles, weather, effort, rest, and grounded resonance.
- Spirit: darkness, stars, thresholds, radiance, silence, surrender, and paradox.

Do not rank the islands. Each has its own Matrix-to-Great-Way sequence expressed as seven spatial encounters or states. Movement among them should reveal feedback: a change made in one subtly alters the others. The full meaning arises through their relationship, not through escaping Body or privileging Spirit.

### 6. Mandala of the Self

Purpose: integration without finality.

Build a responsive three-dimensional mandala from traces of the visitor’s journey. The system may remember only choices made within the current local session; it must not profile, identify, or transmit the visitor. Mind, Body, and Spirit appear as interwoven geometries rather than separate layers. The mandala changes with attention but never grades the visitor or claims to diagnose them. Its final state should feel personal yet unfinished—a mirror of present orientation, not a permanent identity. Let it gradually simplify into a single line, tone, pulse, or field of light, returning the visitor to themselves.

## Technical requirements for every piece

Deliver each artwork as one complete, self-contained HTML file.

- Use Three.js for 3D rendering. The final HTML must run without a build step. Bundle or inline the required Three.js code so the delivered file has no runtime dependency on a CDN.
- Generate all visible content procedurally with code: geometry, particles, linework, textures, shaders, gradients, fog, symbols, and motion.
- Use no external images, video, fonts, models, texture files, audio files, analytics, trackers, network calls, or other external assets.
- Use the attached drawings as visual references only; do not require those image files at runtime unless I explicitly request a later embedded treatment.
- Create a generative ambient soundscape with the Web Audio API. It should be sparse, warm, slow, and responsive, using synthesis rather than samples. Audio begins only after a user gesture. Include an obvious but unobtrusive mute/unmute control.
- Keep the interface minimal. Favor direct interaction, one-line invitations, subtle symbols, and progressive discovery over panels and menus.
- Make each piece responsive and mobile-friendly, including touch, pointer, keyboard, and viewport/orientation changes. Prevent accidental page scrolling during intentional canvas gestures while preserving accessibility.
- Support `prefers-reduced-motion`. Also provide an in-experience reduced-motion control. In reduced mode, remove rapid camera travel, strong parallax, flashes, and continuous disorienting motion while preserving meaning through fades, state changes, and sound.
- Avoid flashing, sudden loud sounds, forced full-screen behavior, aggressive camera rotation, and motion-sickness-inducing acceleration.
- Provide keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, semantic button labels, sufficient contrast, and concise screen-reader instructions. Never encode the entire experience exclusively in color or sound.
- Pause or substantially reduce rendering and audio work when the tab is hidden. Respect mobile battery and thermal constraints.
- Aim for smooth performance on a recent mid-range phone. Adapt particle count, pixel ratio, post-processing, and shader complexity to device capability.
- Include graceful WebGL and Web Audio fallbacks. A fallback may be simpler, but it should retain the contemplative intention.
- Keep source code organized inside the single file with clearly marked sections for configuration, state, scene creation, procedural geometry, interaction, audio, accessibility, resize/visibility handling, animation, and cleanup.
- Add short comments around the non-obvious symbolic mappings and major tunable parameters. Do not bury the artwork beneath engineering commentary.
- Store no personal data, make no network requests, and keep all session-state local and ephemeral by default.

## Shared system across the series

Create a restrained series-wide design system:

- a common opening breath or pulse;
- a recurring fine-line glyph that evolves across pieces;
- consistent line weight, glow behavior, fog, and palette logic;
- a small family of synthesized tones whose intervals transform by realm;
- consistent controls for sound, motion, reset, and quiet help;
- optional local-session continuity that works without accounts or tracking;
- a gentle exit ritual that returns the screen toward darkness and stillness.

Reuse a small internal architecture where useful, but do not make all six pieces feel like reskins. Give each one a unique spatial metaphor, interaction, emotional arc, and sonic identity.

## Working method

Approach this as a serious art-and-engineering collaboration, not a one-shot demo generator.

### If starting from scratch

1. Study the attached drawings and write a concise visual-language analysis: recurring forms, line behavior, density, rhythm, use of text, symmetry/asymmetry, and emotional effect.
2. Translate the source framework into an experience map. For each archetypal station, state the experiential relationship to evoke and a possible nonverbal interaction. Mark source-grounded interpretation separately from artistic invention.
3. Define the shared visual, sonic, interaction, accessibility, and performance system for the series.
4. Create a compact concept sheet for all six pieces: purpose, visitor arc, spatial metaphor, primary interaction, sonic behavior, source relationship, and unique technical challenge.
5. Build the pieces sequentially. Start with Ether Crossing as the foundation. After each build, test it, report what actually works, identify weak points honestly, and carry reusable improvements into the next piece.
6. Preserve editable source and maintain a short change log. Do not claim a piece is complete until its core interaction, audio, mobile behavior, and reduced-motion path have been exercised.

### If continuing existing work

1. Inspect every supplied HTML file before changing it.
2. Summarize the current experience, code structure, strengths, defects, missing requirements, and opportunities to align it more closely with this vision.
3. Preserve what is already strong. Refactor only when it improves reliability, coherence, accessibility, performance, or the contemplative effect.
4. Do not replace a working piece with a generic rewrite merely because you would structure it differently.
5. Return the complete revised self-contained HTML, not patches or fragments.
6. State exactly what changed and what remains unresolved.

### Creative decision rule

For every major choice, ask:

- Does this deepen inward attention, or merely add spectacle?
- Is the symbolism experienced, or merely displayed?
- Does the interaction preserve freedom and mystery?
- Does the art invite unconditional regard without dictating belief?
- Does it feel connected to the hand-drawn source language?
- Does it remain calm, legible, and usable on a phone?

If a feature is visually impressive but pulls attention outward, simplifies the archetypes into clichés, or makes the visitor perform for the system, remove it.

## Definition of done

A piece is done only when:

- it opens as a single HTML file and makes no network requests;
- its main experience works without missing assets;
- the primary interaction has a clear contemplative purpose;
- it supports pointer, touch, and keyboard use;
- audio starts only after consent and can be muted;
- reduced-motion mode preserves the experience’s meaning;
- there are no obvious console errors, broken controls, unreadable overlays, accidental scrolling traps, or severe mobile performance problems;
- it feels recognizably part of this series while remaining distinct;
- it leaves the visitor quieter, more inward, or more curious—not merely impressed.

## Tone of collaboration

Be imaginative, rigorous, and honest. Do not flatter the concept or pad the work with mystical language. Protect the mystery without becoming vague. Challenge choices that become preachy, derivative, technically fragile, or aesthetically generic. When something is uncertain in the source material, say so. When a technical constraint requires a compromise, explain the tradeoff and choose the option that best preserves the inner experience.

Begin now. First, analyze the attached drawings and any existing files, then present the creative north star and six-piece concept map. After that, build or revise Ether Crossing as the first fully working piece. Continue through the series without losing the shared vision.
```
